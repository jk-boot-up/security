SBOMs and Scanning with syft and grype, Explained

The idea in one sentence

You cannot answer whether you are affected by an advisory without a list of what you ship, and the list is useful only if it is written at every build, scanned again on a schedule and read by a gate that has rules for exceptions.

The 5 acts

1. What a bill of materials answers, and what it does not

The input is the image shop-sbom:2.14.1: the base image eclipse-temurin:21-jre-alpine with log4j-core-2.14.1.jar and log4j-api-2.14.1.jar copied into /app/lib, and the command syft shop-sbom:2.14.1 -o cyclonedx-json. The result is a CycloneDX bill whose subject is shop-sbom:2.14.1, listing the operating system packages as pkg:apk/... and the libraries log4j-api@2.14.1 and log4j-core@2.14.1. One package URL part by part: pkg:maven/org.apache.logging.log4j/log4j-core@2.14.1 is the type maven, the namespace org.apache.logging.log4j, the name log4j-core and the version 2.14.1. Its fingerprint: the SHA-1 the bill records equals the SHA-1 this program works out from the jar, and the Apache-2.0 licence is named. The repository, the commit and the builder are not in the bill: that is provenance.

2. Scanning it against known advisories

The input is the command grype shop-sbom:2.14.1 -o json and grype's advisory database, built within the last seven days. The result: in log4j, the critical CVE-2021-44228 (log4shell, fixed in 2.15.0) and CVE-2021-45046 (fixed in 2.16.0), and the high CVE-2021-45105 (fixed in 2.17.0). How one match is made: the bill says log4j-core 2.14.1, the advisory CVE-2021-44228 is fixed in 2.15.0, and 2.14.1 is below 2.15.0, so it matches. The same scan from the bill alone (grype sbom:sbom.cdx.json) finds the same advisories in log4j; the rest of the findings are in the base image's operating system packages, and their number changes daily, so it is not printed.

3. Fixing it, and checking that it is fixed

The input: the fixes needed are 2.15.0, 2.16.0 and 2.17.0, so the upgrade must be to at least 2.17.0, and the team builds shop-sbom:2.25.5. The result: comparing the two bills, log4j-api@2.14.1 and log4j-core@2.14.1 are removed and log4j-api@2.25.5 and log4j-core@2.25.5 added; the operating system packages are the same in both. The new scan finds no critical or high advisory in log4j: 3 before, 0 after.

4. A gate that reads the scan

The input is the rule (refuse a critical or high advisory in the store's libraries; a written exception may cover a high one, until its end day) and one exception: CVE-2021-45105 (high), the reason, ending on day 120. The result for six requests: shop-sbom:2.14.1 is refused with 2 critical and 1 high; with the exception on day 100 it is still refused with 2 critical, because an exception cannot cover a critical advisory. shop-sbom:2.16.0 is refused with 1 high; with the exception on day 100 it is deployed with 1 high excepted; on day 130 the exception has run out and it is refused again. shop-sbom:2.25.5 is deployed.

5. The bill

The input is an image whose /app/lib holds the log4j-core-2.14.1.jar renamed blob.dat. The result: syft lists no log4j library and grype finds no advisory, because a scanner sees only what the cataloguer recognises. The bill written for shop-sbom:2.14.1, offered for shop-sbom:2.25.5, does not describe it: write the bill at every build. And a scan cannot see tomorrow's advisories (keep the bills and rescan on a schedule), cannot know whether the store calls the vulnerable code (a VEX statement records that), and cannot say who built the image (signed provenance, in the plain sbom-and-provenance project).

The verdict

Write a bill of materials for every image at build time, keep it with the image, scan it on a schedule with a fresh database, gate on critical and high advisories with exceptions that name a reason and an end date, and know what your cataloguer cannot see.

How to recognise this in code you did not write

  • syft ... -o cyclonedx-json or grype sbom:... in a pipeline.
  • A bom.json attached to a release.
  • A package URL such as pkg:maven/org.apache.logging.log4j/log4j-core@2.14.1.
  • A scanner with thousands of findings in the base image.

Where you have already met this

  • syft or grype as a step in a pipeline.
  • A bom.json or sbom.cdx.json attached to a release.
  • A scanner report with hundreds of findings, most of them in the base image.
  • A .grype.yaml file with ignore rules.
