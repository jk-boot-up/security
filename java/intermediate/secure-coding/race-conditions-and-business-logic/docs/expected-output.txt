ONE. Check, then act: two requests in the gap.
  ana has 100 and sends two withdrawals of 80 at the same moment, from her phone and her laptop
    A reads the balance: 100
    B reads the balance: 100
    A checks that 100 covers 80: yes
    B checks that 100 covers 80: yes
    A writes 20 and pays out 80
    B writes 20 and pays out 80
  A: paid out 80, B: paid out 80; the bank paid out 160 from a balance of 100, and the balance says 20  <- BOTH CHECKS PASSED BEFORE EITHER WROTE
  the same two requests one after the other: A paid out 80, B refused, so a test that sends them in turn never sees the bug
  a single-use coupon checked, applied, then marked used, sent twice at once: discount given 40 instead of 20  <- THE SAME GAP
TWO. Making the check and the write one step.
  with a lock on the account around read, check and write, in the same order of steps:
    A takes the lock ANA-1
    B waits for the lock ANA-1
    A reads the balance: 100
    B waits for the lock ANA-1
    A checks that 100 covers 80: yes
    B waits for the lock ANA-1
    A writes 20, pays out 80 and releases the lock
    B takes the lock ANA-1
    B reads the balance: 20
    B checks that 20 covers 80: no
    B refuses and releases the lock
  A: paid out 80, B: refused; paid out 80, balance 20
  without a lock, with a compare-and-set that writes only if the balance is still what was read:
    A reads the balance: 100
    B reads the balance: 100
    A checks that 100 covers 80: yes
    B checks that 100 covers 80: yes
    A sets 100 to 20 and pays out 80
    B tries to set 100 to 20: the balance changed, start again
    B reads the balance: 20
    B checks that 20 covers 80: no, refused
  A: paid out 80, B: refused; paid out 80, balance 20
  the coupon claimed in one step before the discount: A discount 20, B refused, discount given 20
  the rule that must always hold, the balance never below zero and each coupon used once, now holds in every order of steps
THREE. Sending the same request twice.
  ana pays 300 rent, the reply is lost, and her app sends the request again: without a key, ana has 400 and ben 600  <- PAID TWICE
  with an idempotency key chosen by the app for this payment: first send: moved 300
  the same key again: already done: moved 300; ana has 700 and ben 300
  the same key with a different amount: refused: the key rent-october was used for a different transfer
  a new key is a new payment: moved 300; ana has 400
  the key is claimed in one step before the money moves, so two copies racing each other cannot both run
FOUR. Rules the server must keep, whatever the client sends.
  a careless bank given a transfer of -50 from ana to ben: ana has 1050 and ben 950  <- MONEY TAKEN FROM BEN
  ten transfers of 1 from ana to herself: ana still has 1050 and earns 10 reward points  <- POINTS FOR NOTHING
  the careful bank: -50: refused: the amount must be above zero; to herself: refused: a transfer to the same account; 5000: refused: not enough money
  a cart with boots at 180, three coupons and a total of 1 written by the client:
    careless checkout charges -59  <- THE STORE OWES THE CUSTOMER
    careful checkout charges 160
      the total sent by the client (1) is ignored
      WELCOME20 refused: one coupon per order
      SPRING20 refused: one coupon per order
  an order kept as flags: shipped before payment true, refunded 360 on an order of 180  <- REFUNDED TWICE, NEVER PAID
  the same order as a state machine: ship first: refused: cannot ship an order that is created; pay: paid; ship: shipped; refund: refunded; refund again: refused: cannot refund an order that is refunded
    refunded 180 in all
FIVE. The bill.
  a lock makes requests to one account take turns: in the order above, B spent 3 steps waiting while A held the lock
  two transfers in opposite directions, each locking its own sender first:
    A locks ANA-1
    B locks BEN-2
    A waits for BEN-2
    B waits for ANA-1
    every request is waiting for a lock another one holds: deadlock
  the same two with every lock taken in one order, the smaller account first: deadlock false; A moved 50, B moved 70; ana has 520, ben 480
  two servers, each with its own lock in its own memory, sharing one database: A paid out 80, B paid out 80, paid out 160 from 100  <- A LOCK IN ONE PROCESS DOES NOT STOP ANOTHER
  with more than one server, the guarantee must come from the database: row locks, isolation levels or a version check,
  which is the -with-postgres version of this project
