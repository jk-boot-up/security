ONE. Issue and read: a root, an intermediate, a certificate.
  THE AUTHORITY (made with openssl):
    root          CN=Bank Root CA, O=Bank
                  self-signed yes, CA yes, path length none
    intermediate  CN=Bank Issuing CA, O=Bank
                  signed by the root yes, CA yes, path length 0
    both          key usage keyCertSign and cRLSign
                  the root signs only the intermediate;
                  the intermediate signs every certificate, CRL
                  and OCSP answer below
  THE CERTIFICATE (issued by the intermediate, read in Java):
    names     api.bank.example, payments.bank.example
    usage     digitalSignature; extended serverAuth; CA no
    key       EC 256 bits, signed SHA256withECDSA
    lifetime  30 days
    pointers  OCSP responder yes, CRL distribution point no
  HOW THE CHAIN IS CHECKED (by hand, now):
    1 leaf issuer = intermediate subject        yes
    2 leaf signature, intermediate's key        yes
    3 intermediate issuer = root subject        yes
    4 intermediate signature, root's key        yes
    5 root: self-signed, held by the client     yes
    6 intermediate: a CA, keyCertSign, path 0   yes
    7 the time is inside every validity         yes
  THE CHECKS (by hand, then by the JDK):
    the chain               by hand     the JDK
    with the intermediate   accepted    accepted
    no intermediate         fails at 1  refused (no chain to the root)
    31 days later           fails at 7  refused (expired)
    a day before issue      fails at 7  refused (not yet valid)
TWO. Revocation: OCSP, a CRL and a dead responder.
  WHEN THE STATUS CANNOT BE LEARNED, A CLIENT CAN:
    hard fail  refuse the certificate
    soft fail  accept it anyway
  OCSP: THE CLIENT ASKS THE RESPONDER (openssl ocsp):
    certificate, responder  hard fail         soft fail
    good, up                accepted          accepted
    revoked, up             refused: revoked  refused: revoked
    good, down              refused: unknown  accepted
    revoked, down           refused: unknown  accepted
    revoked, hangs          refused: unknown  accepted
  THE RESULT:
    requests for the 2 good checks       2
    a hanging check waited about 1 s     yes (the JDK default is 15 s)
    revoked, blocked, soft fail accepts  2 of 2
  A CRL: THE CLIENT DOWNLOADS A SIGNED LIST (good for 3600 s):
    the CRL        1 entry, verified with the intermediate's key
    revoked after  api.bank.example; a client with that CRL: accepted
    the next CRL   2 entries; a client with it: refused (revoked)
    2 hours later  next update passed: hard refused, soft accepted
    the gap        a client with the old list accepts for up to 3600 s
  HOW BIG A CRL GETS (openssl ca writes it):
    entries   size      per entry   at 1 Mbit/s
    1,000     20 KB     21 bytes    0.2 s
    10,000    205 KB    21 bytes    1.7 s
    100,000   2120 KB   22 bytes    17.4 s
  HOW ONE ENTRY IS MADE (DER, read from the CRL):
    serial 1000   2 + (2 + 2 serial) + (2 + 13 date) = 21 bytes
    serial 1969F  2 + (2 + 3 serial) + (2 + 13 date) = 22 bytes
    time          size x 8 bits / 1,000,000 bits a second
  100,000 REVOKED, 90,000 OF THEM ALREADY EXPIRED:
    openssl ca -updatedb keeps   2120 KB
    dropped, as RFC 5280 allows  205 KB: short lives, short lists
THREE. Stapling: the server brings the answer.
  THE STAPLE (openssl s_server -status_file):
    what      the OCSP answer, signed and dated, in the handshake
    size      under 512 bytes: yes
  THE RESPONDER UP (a hard-fail client):
    server              verdict   in handshake  to responder
    staples             accepted  1             0
    does not staple     accepted  0             1
                        and the responder sees which site
  THE RESPONDER DOWN:
    does not staple     hard fail refused: unknown, soft fail accepted
    staples             hard fail accepted
    staples, 2 days on  past next update: hard refused: out of date
  THE CERTIFICATE IS REVOKED (the responder up):
    the old staple                accepted, 0 requests to it
    a client asking the responder refused: revoked
    a staple made after it        refused: revoked
    so a staple is as old as      its last refresh (1 day here)
FOUR. Short lives and automatic rotation.
  THE CERTIFICATE (step-ca, ledger.bank.example):
    lifetime    12 seconds (as issued, within a second: yes)
    chain sent  2 certificates
  HOW THE SCHEDULE IS MADE:
    renew at    12 x 2/3 = 8 seconds after issue
    headroom    12 - 8 = 4 seconds for the CA to be away
    alert at    a quarter of the life left: 12 / 4 = 3 seconds
    if stolen   worth 12 seconds at most, not 30 days
  ROTATION (30 seconds, a handshake every 100 ms):
    handshakes  300, failed 0
    renewals    3, failed 0
    alerts      0
    serial      not the one at the start: yes
  THE CA AWAY FOR ABOUT 2 S, JUST BEFORE A RENEWAL:
    handshakes  200 in 20 seconds, failed 0
    renewals    failed while away: yes, done once back: yes
FIVE. The missed renewal, and a monitor.
  THE CA STAYS DOWN PAST THE EXPIRY:
    before the expiry   50 handshakes, 0 refused
    after the expiry    100 handshakes, 100 refused
                        the reason: certificate expired
    renewals            failed, more than 10: yes; done 0
    the monitor         alerted before the first refusal: yes
                        and at least 2 seconds before: yes
  THE CA IS BACK:
    renew the old one   refused yes (it has to be issued anew)
    a new one           20 handshakes, 0 refused
  AN INVENTORY OF 60 CERTIFICATES (the monitor's first scan):
    expired           4
    under 7 days      6
    under 30 days     14
    30 days or more   36
                      4 expired, found before any report
  THE NEXT 60 DAYS (a scan every morning, 26 expire):
    the monitor                   outages   prevented
    none                          26        0
    alert 7 d, owner needs 3 d    3         23
    alert 7 d, owner needs 10 d   26        0
    alert 30 d, owner needs 10 d  8         18
  HOW AN OUTAGE IS COUNTED:
    alert     the first morning with fewer than the alert's days left
    renewed   the alert day + the days the owner needs
    outage    renewed after the expiry: a 7 day alert cannot help
              a team that needs 10 days
