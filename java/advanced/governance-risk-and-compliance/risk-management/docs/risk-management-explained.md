# Risk Management, Explained

## The idea in one sentence

A risk number without a range and a tail is a promise nobody can keep.

## The 5 acts

### 1. Heat map against money

Eight risks have a least, a most likely and a most value for how often they happen and for what an event costs. Their scores (likelihood times impact) rank card breach, insider fraud and outage first, while the simulated expected loss ranks ransomware, third-party breach and card breach first.

### 2. The whole register

50,000 simulated years add up the eight risks. The mean agrees with mean frequency times mean loss, which checks the simulation. The tail is the news: 1 year in 10 loses more than $12.8M. An estimate from 1,000 years is 0.9% away from the one from 50,000.

### 3. Controls within a budget

Seven controls each change the frequency or the loss of some risks. Trying every set within the budget on the same simulated years gives backups, multi-factor login, vendor assessments and dual approval: $610k, expected loss $3.1M, net benefit $2.1M. Buying the cheapest first gives a net benefit that is $581k a year smaller.

### 4. Appetite and what is left

The appetite says at most a 5% chance of losing more than $5.0M in a year. Before: 41.4%. After the best set: 19.5%, still outside. With all seven controls, which cost $1.7M: 4.1%, inside. Reaching the appetite costs more than the budget, so the committee must spend more, transfer the rest with insurance, or change the appetite.

### 5. The bill

A ransomware loss estimate 50% higher moves the expected loss by $116k, a small change next to the uncertainty of the ranges themselves. Real incidents arrive together, which this model does not show. Present the range and the tail, not only the mean.

## The verdict

Keep the register as ranges, simulate, rank by expected loss and by the tail, price controls against the loss they avoid, state the appetite as a number, and show the model's limits with the result.

## How to recognise this in code you did not write

- A heat map with no money on it.
- A control budget set by last year's budget.
- An appetite statement with no number.
- A risk estimate given as one value.

## Where you have already met this

- A heat map where everything is amber.
- A control bought because a peer bought it.
- A risk register updated once a year.
- An appetite statement with no number in it.
