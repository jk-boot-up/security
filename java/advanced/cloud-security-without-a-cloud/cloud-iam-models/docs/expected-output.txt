TWO. Accounts: who has to agree.
  ben in the bank account, the bucket in the same account; identity policy yes bucket policy yes -> ALLOW (allowed by an identity policy)
  ben in the bank account, the bucket in the same account; identity policy yes bucket policy no  -> ALLOW (allowed by an identity policy)
  ben in the bank account, the bucket in the same account; identity policy no  bucket policy yes -> ALLOW (allowed by the resource policy)
  ben in the bank account, the bucket in the other account; identity policy yes bucket policy yes -> ALLOW (allowed by both accounts)
  ben in the bank account, the bucket in the other account; identity policy yes bucket policy no  -> DENY  (implicit deny: the resource's account does not allow it)
  ben in the bank account, the bucket in the other account; identity policy no  bucket policy yes -> DENY  (implicit deny: the principal's own account does not allow it)
  in one account either side is enough; across accounts both must say yes, so one account cannot hand out another's data, or take it
  a bucket policy with Principal *: someone with no account at all can read: true  <- EVERYONE ON THE INTERNET
  a stranger from another account whose own policies allow nothing: false, and with a policy of their own that allows it: true
  an organization policy that does not allow s3:GetObject stops even an administrator: DENY  (implicit deny: no allow in the organization policies)
THREE. Roles and the layers that narrow them.
  ben assumes Developer, whose trust policy asks for a second factor: without it DENY  (implicit deny: the trust policy does not allow it); with it ALLOW (the trust policy and the caller's policies allow it)
  eight actions asked of the session; the role's own policy allows 6: [s3:GetObject, s3:PutObject, s3:DeleteObject, dynamodb:Query, ec2:RunInstances, iam:PassRole]
  with a permission boundary of s3:* and dynamodb:*, only what both allow is left: 4: [s3:GetObject, s3:PutObject, s3:DeleteObject, dynamodb:Query]
  and a session policy passed at assumption, narrower again: 2: [s3:GetObject, dynamodb:Query]
  a session policy can only narrow: asking it for iam:CreateUser as well changes nothing: DENY  (implicit deny: outside the permission boundary)
  a bucket policy that names the session itself is not narrowed by the boundary or the session policy: s3:DeleteObject ALLOW (the resource policy names the principal)  <- NAMING A PRINCIPAL IS A STRONGER GRANT THAN NAMING AN ACCOUNT
  conditions: the payments team from the office network true, from elsewhere false, another team from the office false
